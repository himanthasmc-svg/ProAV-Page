AV Wall Structure & Pattress Engineering Design

Target Room:
FOS = 4.0 Standard Enforced

Structural engineering and wall strengthening suite for LED video walls and heavy commercial displays. Calculates full design load with Factor of Safety (FOS = 4.0), overturning moment, anchor tensile pull-out, shear, and pattress sizing across concrete, masonry, light gauge metal stud, timber stud, CMU, and Unistrut grids. Includes interactive dual-view cross-section & elevation diagrams.

Wall Structure & Pattress Engineering Guide

Structural Rationale: Commercial AV displays and LED video walls exert significant dead loads and forward cantilever moments on partition walls. Standard 12.5mm plasterboard has virtually zero tensile pull-out strength. Factor of Safety 4.0 is mandatory in commercial building codes to prevent catastrophic structural collapse.

  • Factor of Safety (FOS 4.0): Design load is calculated as 4× total equipment mass ($W \times 4.0$) in accordance with international rigging standards (ANSI E1.21 / Eurocode 3).
  • Cantilever Moment ($M_o$): Calculated from the horizontal offset of the equipment Center of Gravity (CoG) to the wall mounting plane.
  • Pattress Plate Sizing: In framed drywall, 18mm or 25mm structural marine plywood must span across ≥ 3 vertical studs with perimeter fixings at 150mm centers to distribute point loads into the building structure.
  • Elevation AFFL: Floor line is benchmarked at AFFL 0.0 mm. Display clearance height, centerline height, and top of screen are precisely calculated.

Camera Column: What are D1 & Span? Optical Geometry

In video conference design, the camera's Horizontal Field of View (HFOV) must be wide enough to capture all participants in the first row without cutting off their shoulders.

D1 (Distance to 1st Row): Front wall camera lens to closest participant row (m). Typical: 1.2m–1.8m (Huddle), 2.0m–2.8m (Medium), 3.0m–3.8m (Boardroom). Shorter D1 demands wider HFOV.
Span (Wspan - Seating Width): Horizontal width across table occupied by 1st row viewers (m). Standard 2-person span = 1.6m–1.8m; 3-person span = 2.2m–2.6m.
Visual Example: For D1 = 2.0m and Span = 2.2m:
• Half-Angle θ = arctan(1.1 / 2.0) = 28.8° → HFOV = 57.6° (Recommends 85° PTZ Camera).
• If D1 is reduced to 1.3m (Huddle space), HFOV jumps to 80.4° (Recommends 120° ePTZ Video Bar).

Speaker Count: Why is 2.5" count LESS than 6.5"? Dispersion Physics
Yes, it is 100% mathematically and acoustically correct!

In ceiling distributed audio, required speaker count is inversely proportional to the conical dispersion angle (θ) of the driver cone:

• 2.5" Micro-Cone (θ = 150°): Acts as a point-source with an ultra-wide 150° cone (half-angle 75°). At seated ear level (1.2m AFFL), floor coverage radius is R = Hrel × tan(75°) ≈ 3.73 × Hrel, covering ~43.7 × Hrel² of floor area per speaker.
• 6.5" Driver (θ = 100°): Has narrower directional beaming (half-angle 50°). Floor coverage radius is R = Hrel × tan(50°) ≈ 1.19 × Hrel, covering only ~4.46 × Hrel² of floor area.
• Geometric Result: A single 2.5" speaker covers nearly 9.8× more floor area than a 6.5" speaker! Thus, fewer 2.5" units are needed geometrically for edge-to-edge sound coverage.
Acoustic Purpose: 2.5" speakers excel at broad speech intelligibility (150Hz–18kHz). 6.5" drivers deliver deeper bass (65Hz–20kHz) and higher SPL headroom, but require tighter grid spacing to eliminate dropout notches.

PARAMETER 1
What is D1 (Distance to 1st Row)?

D1 is the physical distance (in meters or feet) from the camera lens located on the front wall to the first row of participants seated at the conference table.

  • Why it matters: Shorter D1 demands a wider lens angle so that people seated on the left and right ends aren't cropped out.
  • Huddle Rooms: D1 is typically 1.0m to 1.6m. Demands 110°–120° wide ePTZ video bars.
  • Medium Rooms: D1 is typically 2.0m to 2.8m. Standard 80°–85° PTZ camera lenses work perfectly.
  • Boardrooms: D1 is typically 2.8m to 3.8m. Requires 70°–75° PTZ cameras with 12x optical zoom.
Rule: If D1 < 1.8m, always specify an Ultra-Wide ePTZ Video Bar (120° HFOV).
PARAMETER 2
What is Span (Wspan - Seating Width)?

Span (Wspan) is the horizontal width across the conference table occupied by the participants in the first row who must be framed simultaneously.

  • Why it matters: If the seating span exceeds the camera's optical coverage width at D1, the matrix table highlights the row in Red.
  • 2-Person Front Row Span: Approximately 1.6m to 1.8m (each participant occupies ~0.8m–0.9m).
  • 3-Person Front Row Span: Approximately 2.2m to 2.6m wide.
  • Wide U-Shape / Executive Span: Approximately 3.0m to 4.5m wide.
Formula: θ = arctan((Span / 2) ÷ D1) → HFOV = 2 × θ.

CATEGORY 1
Ceiling Beamforming Array (MXA920 / TCC2)

Flush ceiling tiles utilizing hundreds of tiny MEMS capsules and DSP to generate steerable directional lobes that dynamically track talkers across the table.

  • Coverage Range: Up to 9m × 9m (30ft × 30ft) per ceiling array tile.
  • Advantage: Zero cables or clutter on the conference table; automatic tracking of standing or pacing speakers.
  • Best For: Executive Boardrooms, Video Training Labs, Divisible Meeting Halls.
CATEGORY 2
Tabletop Boundary Microphones

Placed flat directly on the table surface. Operates in the acoustic pressure boundary zone, which delivers +3dB acoustic gain with zero phase cancellation from table reflections.

  • Coverage Ratio: 1 boundary mic per 4 to 6 seated participants (pickup radius ~1.5m–2.0m).
  • Advantage: Extremely natural vocal pickup at close range, compact low-profile footprint.
  • Best For: Huddle spaces, small meeting rooms, and round table collaboration.
CATEGORY 3
Table Gooseneck Microphones

Individual stalk-mounted hypercardioid capsules bringing the mic element within 15cm–25cm of each speaker's mouth.

  • Coverage Ratio: 1 dedicated gooseneck per participant seat.
  • Advantage: Highest possible Gain-Before-Feedback (GBF), private local mute touch-ring on every station.
  • Best For: Government Chambers, Legal Hearing Rooms, VIP Boardrooms.
CATEGORY 4
Video Bar Integrated Mic Arrays

Multi-element beamforming microphone array integrated directly into the front all-in-one video bar beneath the display (Logitech MeetUp, Poly Studio, Cisco Room Bar).

  • Coverage Reach: Effective voice pickup up to 3.5m to 4.5m from front wall.
  • Advantage: Single-device simplicity, zero room trenching or ceiling conduit required.
  • Best For: Focus rooms, 2–6 person huddles, informal team breakout booths.

Acoustic Physics

Why is the 2.5" speaker count LESS than 6.5"? Is it correct?
Yes, it is 100% mathematically and acoustically correct! The count difference is governed by conical acoustic dispersion angle (θ):
• 2.5" Micro-Cone (θ = 150°): Behaves like a point-source radiator with an ultra-wide 150° dispersion cone (half-angle 75°). At seated ear height (1.2m AFFL, relative height Hrel = H - 1.2m), its floor coverage radius is R = Hrel × tan(75°) ≈ 3.73 × Hrel. A single 2.5" cone covers ~43.7 × Hrel² of floor area.
• 6.5" Coaxial Driver (θ = 100°): Features directional beaming with a narrower 100° cone (half-angle 50°). Its coverage radius is R = Hrel × tan(50°) ≈ 1.19 × Hrel, covering only ~4.46 × Hrel² of floor area.
• Coverage Area Ratio: One 2.5" speaker covers nearly 9.8× more floor area than a 6.5" speaker! Consequently, fewer 2.5" units are needed geometrically for basic edge-to-edge speech coverage.
Design Trade-off: 2.5" provides broad speech intelligibility (150Hz–18kHz, ~88dB SPL). 6.5" delivers higher SPL headroom (~104dB SPL) and deep bass extension (65Hz–20kHz, music/program audio), requiring closer spacing to avoid dropout notches.

4. MOUNTING PLACEMENT & ERGONOMIC SIGHTLINE Eye-Level Standard
⬇️ Mounted Below Display (Eye-Level 1.2m Sightline)

When the display bottom is at standard eye-height clearance (850–950mm AFFL), mounting below display positions camera lens closest to participant eye level (1.2m), avoiding the unflattering look-down angle (≤ 15° tilt).
